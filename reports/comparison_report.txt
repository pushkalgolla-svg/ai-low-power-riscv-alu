[ENGINEERING ANALYSIS & ARCHITECTURAL VERDICT]
1. Dynamic Switching Activity Suppression:
   - Arithmetic datapath inputs achieved -41.6% transition toggle reduction (-327 toggles).
   - Logic datapath inputs achieved -23.1% transition toggle reduction (-182 toggles).
   - Operator-level boundary: In unshared architectures with separate adder and subtractor units, total arithmetic
     operator transitions dropped by -70.8% due to resource sharing and input gating.
   - Mechanism: Inputs to the 8-bit carry chain are gated to zero during logical operations,
     suppressing dynamic switching activity across inactive functional sub-blocks.
2. Resource-Shared Arithmetic Architecture:
   - Unified 9-bit two's-complement arithmetic unit replaces separate addition and subtraction operators.
   - Subtraction inverts operand B (b_operand = ~arith_b) and asserts carry-in (cin = 1).
   - Borrow flag is extracted via carry-out inversion (~arith_res[8]).
3. Logic Cell Complexity & Netlist Interconnect:
   - Generic cell count reduced by -3.0%: Unified adder reduces arithmetic cell count,
     achieving net cell savings despite input gating multiplexer insertion.
   - Netlist wires (nets) reduced by -19.3%: Reduced multiplexing stages and resource sharing
     minimize internal netlist interconnect footprint.
4. Timing Limitations & Target Technology Disclaimer:
   - Critical Path Delay: Gating multiplexers introduce one 2-to-1 MUX gate delay on operand arrival.
     The 8-bit ripple carry chain dominates the critical path, so gating has minimal timing overhead.

     transitions) and generic CMOS technology-independent netlist complexity (Yosys synth -noabc).
